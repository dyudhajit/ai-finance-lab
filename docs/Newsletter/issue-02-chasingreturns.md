# Issue #2 — Chasing Returns

## Why the Next Dollar a Company Invests Determines Its Future Value

**Published: August 2026**

---

## Overview

Growth is one of the most misunderstood concepts in finance.

A company can increase revenue, expand operations, and report higher earnings while simultaneously destroying shareholder value.

The critical question is not **how much a business grows**.

The critical question is **what return it earns on the capital required to achieve that growth**.

This issue introduces return on capital as the central framework for evaluating business quality and long-term value creation.

---

## Key Ideas

### 1. Growth Does Not Automatically Create Value

Every investment made by a company has a cost.

When management invests in:

* factories,
* inventory,
* acquisitions,
* technology,
* or expansion,

that capital must generate an attractive return.

Growth only creates value when the returns earned on new investments exceed the company’s cost of capital.

---

### 2. The Return That Matters Is the Next One

Historical return on capital tells us how successful a business has been.

The more important question is:

> **What return will the company earn on the next dollar it invests?**

This is the idea of **incremental return on capital**.

Many companies maintain impressive historical returns while new investments generate lower and lower returns.

Understanding this difference is essential for evaluating future value creation.

---

### 3. Capital Allocation Is the Hidden Engine of Compounding

Great businesses share one characteristic:

They can reinvest capital at high returns for long periods of time.

This creates a compounding engine that allows them to:

* grow earnings,
* generate cash,
* fund expansion,
* and increase intrinsic value.

The best investors spend as much time evaluating **capital allocation** as they do evaluating profitability.

---

### 4. The 4MATR Return Framework

When analysing any business, focus on four questions:

| Question                                      | Why It Matters                             |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| How much capital is being invested?           | Measures the scale of reinvestment         |
| How much operating profit is being generated? | Measures economic productivity             |
| Are returns improving or declining?           | Identifies changes in business quality     |
| Can high returns be sustained?                | Determines long-term compounding potential |

This framework is fundamental to valuation, equity research, private equity, and long-term investing.
